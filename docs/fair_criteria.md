# Judging criteria for Synopsys, CSEF and ISEF (checked 2026-11-05)

Paraphrased from the official pages. Re-check each page before the fair in case it changes.

## Synopsys Championship (Santa Clara Valley), category judging

Source: https://science-fair.org/judges-3/category-judges/judging-criteria/ and https://science-fair.org/judging-faq-for-students/

Four criteria, 10 points each, 40 in total. The same criteria apply in every category; engineering projects are judged on
engineering goals instead of scientific thought.

| Criterion (10 each) | What judges check |
|---|---|
| Scientific Thought / Engineering Goal | Significant problem, clear hypothesis (or clear design criteria); problem well limited and planned; variables controlled; enough literature research, and others' work considered; conclusion justified by the data; testing, redesign and retesting; knows what further research is needed. Engineering: is the software workable and feasible, could it be used in a real product or program? |
| Creativity | Unique topic or original approach; novel way of testing the hypothesis. Ideas from the internet or elsewhere are fine if acknowledged but score lower. How much help did the student get with the idea and execution? |
| Independent Work / Skill | Students did the work and credit mentors; their role in designing and programming; understanding fits the grade level; good technical, data and programming skill. |
| Thoroughness / Clarity | Scope fully covered; enough data; repeated tests; claims backed by data; **project notebook kept (required, grades 9-12)**; **all work completed in the past 12 months**; procedures documented; a software demo helps; clear answers; clear board and abstract; phases presented in order. |

Interview format (FAQ): a one to two minute spoken summary with no charts or formal presentation, then questions. Judges
may look at the notebook and ask about the abstract. Judges pick about 40 % of projects in a group for category awards.
Grand Prize judging (grades 9-12) chooses who goes to ISEF; a first place category award does not guarantee CSEF or ISEF.

## California Science & Engineering Fair (CSEF)

Source: https://californiascienceandengineeringfair.tawk.help/article/the-judging-process (help article about 5 years
old). The main site, csef.usc.edu, would not load (certificate error), so the current CSEF rules have not been checked.

- Students submit a project presentation PDF, and may add one video link and one link to a folder or code repository.
- Category judges use these for a first round, which can cut projects. Finalists then get a timed interview, and judges
  may stop a prepared talk to ask questions.
- **No numerical rubric.** Winners are chosen by strong agreement of the category panel after discussion.
- Sponsored awards are judged separately by each sponsor.

## Regeneron ISEF, Grand Award criteria

Source: https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/ (no PDF guide or rules book is linked from it).

Science rubric, 100 points:

| Section | Points | What judges check |
|---|---|---|
| I. Research Question | 10 | Clear, focused purpose; contribution to the field; testable by scientific methods |
| II. Design and Methodology | 15 | Well-designed plan and data collection; variables and controls defined, appropriate and complete |
| III. Execution: data collection, analysis, interpretation | 20 | Systematic collection and analysis; reproducible; suitable maths and statistics; enough data to support the conclusions |
| IV. Creativity and Potential Impact | 20 | Real creativity in one or more parts; impact on the field or on society, technology, economy or environment |
| V. Presentation | 35 | Poster 10 (logical layout, clear graphics, supporting documents); interview 25 (clear answers, understanding of the basic science, of the limits of the results, independence, potential impact, ideas for further research; for teams, every member's contribution and understanding) |

The engineering rubric replaces I with Research Problem (a practical need, criteria for a solution, constraints), II with
alternatives explored, a solution chosen and a prototype or model built, and III with Construction and Testing (the
prototype works as designed, tested in several conditions). IV and V are the same.

Other ISEF rules on the page: judges look at the notebook and any forms; continuing projects need Form 7 and are judged
only on this year's new work; teams and individuals are judged together, and every team member must show real
contribution and understanding.

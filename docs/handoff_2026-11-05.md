## 4. What has been built and found

### 4.1 Travel time model
- LightGBM error is 45.5 s, against 53.8 s for the Kolesar curve. Figures and poster were updated to show 45.5 s.

### 4.2 Neutral test (section 6.1 of `results.md`)
- Hidden real stations are picked by each model and scored on recorded trips.
- LightGBM's edge over road or straight-line Kolesar is under 1 s. Any model beats random picks by 3–4 s, so the evidence for the original hypothesis is weak.
- **Rule from this:** a layout only counts as better if it wins under all three models (LightGBM, road, Kolesar).

### 4.3 2014 closures (section 3)
- **Overall effect:** the recorded effect was +70.5 s. The busy-engine LightGBM model predicted +67.5 s, and road Kolesar +71.0 s.
- **Which areas were hit hardest:** the dispatch model ranks the 10 areas at r = 0.59, with an error of 13.0 s per area. The ranking of which closed station to reopen is not validated: with 10 stations it can't be told apart from chance.

### 4.4 Existing recommended layouts (section 5)

### 4.5 The first-engine "gap" (section 2b)
- **Definition:** another station arrived first, the local station was closer by road, and its engine looked free. This covers about 13–14 % of calls, which take +148 s (2024).
- **Upper limit if fully removed:** 19.5 s on average, or 6.3 over-6-minute points (about 8,000 calls a year).
- **By time of day:** midday 21.9 % versus night 5.6 %.
- **Two-state hidden model (Baum-Welch, 15-minute grid, per engine count):**
  - It predicts hidden calls with AUC 0.72 against 0.66 for the baseline, in every year from 2021 to 2024. Only 4.8 % of hidden calls are predicted above 40 %.
  - One-engine stations rarely make trips to other areas during these windows, a drop of 53–69 % after correcting for bias with simulated labels.
  - Windows usually start between 09:00 and 11:00, and the first-to-last gap has a median of 2 hours.
- **14-day cycle:** it showed up in all 5 years. A fixed-in-advance prediction was tested once on 2025 and **FAILED as written**: the 14-day part held (+5.29) but the 7-day part did not (+0.99, needed ≤ 0). A stricter claim needs a new prediction on 2026. Copy the prediction and the failed result into the notebook.
- **Neighbouring stations:** a gap often follows a neighbour's gap (+7.5 to 12.2 points against +0.5 to 2.8 for distant pairs). Passed-over next-nearest stations are worth only 2–4 s even if fixed.
- **Moving away time to quieter hours:** saves 0–1 s within realistic hours. Cutting away time by 25 % saves 2.75 s, and by 50 % saves 5.5 s. Section 2b.5.
- **What the gap follows:** the station (station identity predicts a held-out year at r = 0.81), not workload. Engine count explains R² 0.42, but that isn't causal. Moving a station probably would not remove it (slope 0.88). The cause is unknown, and there is no crew data.
- **Hidden delay:** with gap calls removed, the 2025 average goes from 329 s to 311 s. With empty-station calls also removed, it is 302 s.
- **Email additions suggested:**
  - Replace the "one to four hours" sentence with "runs lasting several hours…, repeat 14 days later more than 7 days later".
  - Ask: "Is there a rota that repeats every 14 days? Are neighbouring stations ever away at the same time by design?"

### 4.6 Placement methods screen (section 5.1, `placement_methods.py`)
- **Search used:** best-swap search with no floors. It is weaker than the exact planner.
- **At 10 moves:** average −4.9 s, over-6-minute objective −4.5 s, away-aware −4.6 s, many more sites −5.3 s.
- **Many more sites:** they help more as moves increase (−12.9 s at 53 moved, still under the search).
- **Moving engines only:** about 1 s.
- **Section 5.2:** this was added separately, by Ricky or another session, with `placement_analysis.py`. It shows that savings are stable across chosen years, a land-use check with OpenStreetMap rules A, B and C, and that Move 10 plus 9–10 engine moves adds 0.9–1.4 s.

### 4.7 Limit of Move 10 (section 5.3, `move10_limit.py`, `move10_polish.py`)
- **With every protection removed:** the best Move 10 found is about −6.2 s on 2025, or −7.0 s on 2024 after street refinement. Three different starting layouts converge there, but this isn't proven optimal.
- **Rural-to-urban idea:** this was Ricky's idea, and it is the **worst option**:
  - Close the 10 quietest stations and open sites in the centre: −1.2 to −1.6 s, with the slowest 10 % of calls 35 s worse.
  - Move outer stations to the centre: −1.7 s, and the solver stops at 6–8 moves.
  - Reason: central London is already dense, and outer calls have no nearby backup.
- **Ceiling:** adding 10 stations with 2 new engines each and closing none gives −13.9 s, with 0 % of calls 30+ s slower. Closing the 10 old stations costs about 7 s.
- **Conclusion:** Move 10 is effectively capped at about 6–7 s. Adding engine moves might reach about 7 s, but this is untested on the real layout.

### 4.8 Exact planner on wider sites (section 5.4)
- **Settings:** 989 sites, 1 s borough floor plus every other floor, run with `recommended_placement.py` and new flags.
- **Results:** 10 moved −5.8 s, 20 moved −8.1 s, 30 moved −12.8 s, 46 moved −18.0 s, 64 moved (redesign) −20.6 s.
- **Not proven optimal:** all of these came from the fallback search. The 20-move layout probably isn't the best possible.

### 4.9 Rescore under three models and land rules (section 5.5, `score_wide_layouts.py`)

| Moved | LightGBM | Road | Kolesar | Rule B (LightGBM / road / Kolesar) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | −5.8 | −4.6 | −2.6 | −5.2 / −4.2 / −2.5 |
| 20 | −8.1 | −7.8 | −4.6 | −8.0 / −7.7 / −4.8 |
| 30 | −12.8 | −10.7 | −6.2 | −11.5 / −10.1 / −6.3 |
| 46 | −18.0 | −15.0 | −8.6 | −16.7 / −14.2 / −8.4 |
| 64 | −20.6 | −17.2 | −8.6 | −18.1 / −15.6 / −8.9 |

- **Every layout wins** under all three models.
- **Road keeps 79–97 % of the LightGBM saving, and Kolesar keeps 42–57 %.** LightGBM is likely the most favourable because it chose the sites.
- **Redesign versus 46 moves:** the redesign adds nothing over 46 moves under Kolesar. So **46 moves looks best**; quote about 8–17 s, with road (about 14–15 s) as the fair central figure.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - The 1 s borough floor holds only under LightGBM. Under the other models, up to 5 boroughs get 1+ s worse, by up to about 10 s.
  - At 46 moves, 8.5–11 % of calls get 30+ s slower, while 29 % get 30+ s faster. The slowest 10 % still improve under all three models.
- **Land rules:**
  - **Rule A** (no parks, water or schools, road access) changes almost nothing.
  - **Rule B** (plus a commercial or industrial plot within 300 m) costs up to about 2.5 s.
  - **Rule C** (plus the site itself not residential) is over-penalised, because failing sites are moved up to 3.3 km without re-optimising.
- **Uncertainty:** the 95 % ranges are about ±0.25 s but cover only call sampling. The model spread is the real uncertainty.

## 5. Key reasoning behind decisions

- **Why three models:** a layout chosen with one model always looks good under it.
- **Why a simulated baseline:** several tests, such as the trips test, were biased by the method itself. A simulated baseline exposes that bias.
- **Why no claimed cause for the gap:** there is no crew or duty data. LFB's reply may explain it.
- **Why the 19.5 s can't be claimed as a saving:** it's a ceiling. A realistic 25 % cut in away time gives about 3 s.
- **Why choose areas in advance:** quoting the best boroughs after seeing results (the −27 s) is cherry-picking. The target rule must be fixed first.
- **Why area focus doesn't add savings:** it reframes, and the London-wide saving drops (about 6 s to about 4 s with a 0 s borough floor). It does make the local benefit visible. (The 2.3 s figure compares the planner's Move 10 without and with a 0 s borough floor. The figure to quote is 1.8 s, from `docs/research_question_evaluation.md` and section 5.6 of `results.md`, which compares the targeted and citywide searches under the same limits.)
- **Why the 2026 test is held back:** it is the strongest evidence, so use it once, with predictions committed first to timestamp them.

## 6. Research-question options

3. **C. How much average speed does a city give up so that no area gets worse?** About 2.3 s of 6 s at 10 moves.
4. **D. Does moving stations help poorer neighbourhoods most?** Poorest −9.6 s against richest −5.5 s, but this was found after looking, not predicted.
5. **E. How much of the delay can moving stations fix?** Solid but mostly a "limits" message.
6. **F. Can models predict real closure damage?** The 2014 test. Better as supporting evidence.
7. **G. Does a better travel model give better layouts?** Rigorous, with a surprising result (under 1 s difference), but low impact.
8. **H. Software that finds the fewest moves to meet an area's target.** An engineering framing; it needs real redesign and retesting.
9. **I. Keep the current question.** Least work, low creativity.

**Since the rescore (4.9)**, "How many stations must London move before it matters?" is well supported: about 2.5–6 s at 10 moves and about 8–17 s at 46. The fair choice between that and A needs A's targeted-area results put through the same three models and land rules first.
